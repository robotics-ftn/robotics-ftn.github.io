        Locomotion research at FTN centres on biped walking with reconfigurable motion primitives, dynamic balance with multiple contacts, and online path planning when the terrain is not known in advance.

        Core papers include realization of biped walking in unstructured environments ([IEEE T-RO, 2014](/publications/biped-motion-primitives-2014/)), human-inspired online path planning with walking primitives ([Frontiers in Neurorobotics, 2019](/publications/human-inspired-path-planning-2019/)), and a review of the zero-moment point after fifty-five years ([IJHR, 2024](/publications/zmp-fifty-five-years-2024/)). Related work covers disturbance compensation, sliding prevention, and the MARKO assistive humanoid.
